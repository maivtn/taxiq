01 The story

From scattered papers to a record that is easier to review.

At the end of a busy salon day, income details, expense records and receipts may sit in different places. Bringing them together is the first step toward understanding what is on hand and what still needs to be checked.

A nail professional could begin with one income or expense: describe the work, enter an amount and select a receipt. The TAX IQ direction is to help surface questions and trusted tax guides before a conversation with a qualified professional.     03 A PRACTICAL TAX CHECK

    What may count as a business deduction?

    Salon owners and genuinely self-employed nail technicians carry different business costs. Choose the view that fits your work, then check each expense against its business purpose and records before filing.

    SALON OWNER

    See the whole business.

    Salon rent & utilities

    Business rent, electricity, water and internet paid for the salon may need review; separate any personal share.

    Service products & inventory

    Polish, disposables and products used in services differ from merchandise held for resale, which can require inventory or cost-of-goods-sold treatment.

    Employees & contract labor

    Wages and payments for actual business services are distinct categories. Confirm worker classification and information-reporting duties.

    Insurance & licenses

    Business liability coverage and recurring business permits or regulatory fees may be considered with their supporting records.

    Marketing, POS & admin

    Business advertising, payment-processing and bank fees, software, bookkeeping and business tax advice may be relevant when ordinary and necessary.

    Equipment & repairs

    A repair differs from a new equipment purchase or improvement; larger assets may need capitalization, depreciation or another permitted election.

    INDEPENDENT NAIL TECH

    See the costs you actually carry.

    Booth or workspace rent

    Rent you actually pay for a place to perform independent services may be reviewed with the agreement and payment record.

    Products you purchase

    Materials and disposable supplies you buy for client services may be business costs; track what you paid and how items were used.

    Licenses & skill training

    Business license fees and education that maintains or improves skills in your current trade may qualify; training for a new trade follows different rules.

    Coverage, booking & fees

    Business insurance, booking and payment fees, plus advice on your business taxes, may be relevant when you personally pay them for independent work.

    Phone & business travel

    Review only the documented business share of mixed-use phone or vehicle costs. Travel from home to a regular workplace is generally commuting.

    Tools & equipment

    Tools you buy for your own services may need review; expensive equipment may follow depreciation or other asset rules.

    Examples for review, not automatic deductions. Who paid, how the item is used, worker status and supporting records all matter.

    DO NOT COUNT THE WHOLE AMOUNT

    Personal spending

    Personal or family costs generally are not business deductions.

    Daily commute

    The trip between home and a regular workplace is generally commuting, even if you use your own car.

    Mixed-use costs

    For a shared vehicle or similar expense, document and review only the business portion.

    Separate before you claim

    Personal and family spending is generally not a business deduction. Shared costs require a supportable business portion. An item without enough records should be flagged for review, not marked deductible. TAX IQ is a proposed way to organize these questions; it does not decide tax eligibility.

    IRS guidance: Publication 334 · Schedule C · Travel & car expenses · Work-related education · Worker classification · Recordkeeping
